# Policy research — tree of heaven (Ailanthus altissima), Denver/Colorado
Researched 2026-10-08. Factual summary only; no recommendations.

## 1. Colorado noxious weed list — tree of heaven status

**Tree of heaven is NOT on Colorado's List A, B, or C.** It is on the state's **Watch List**
(advisory only — no legal control requirement). The Colorado Department of Agriculture's
"Weeds 101" page names it explicitly: "A plant like Tree-of-heaven is on the Watch List."

- Watch List purpose: advise/educate the public and land managers about potential threats;
  encourage identification and reporting of new populations (to EDDMapS) so the state can decide
  whether the species should be designated A, B, or C. No legal mandate to control.
- The official lists live in the Rules Pertaining to the Administration and Enforcement of the
  Colorado Noxious Weed Act, 8 CCR 1206-2.

**What the lists mean (per CDA):**
- **List A — Eradication (highest priority):** newly arrived / uncommon / limited populations.
  Mandatory eradication on ALL public and private lands statewide; it is a violation to allow
  these species to produce seed or reproduce.
- **List B — Eradication / containment / suppression:** discrete statewide distributions;
  well-established in some areas, limited in others. Mandatory eradication or containment on all
  public and private lands (objectives vary by county); violation to allow seed/reproduction.
- **List C — Widespread / well-established:** control is recommended but NOT required by the
  state; local governments may choose to require management.
- All species on Lists A, B, and C: illegal to sell, offer for sale, or intentionally grow in Colorado.

**Plausible placement if listed:** List B. List A is for rare/newly arrived species (does not fit —
tree of heaven is entrenched in the Denver metro). List C would create no mandates, defeating the
policy goal. List B's "discrete statewide distribution" criterion fits a species concentrated in
urban areas rather than statewide.

Sources:

- https://ag.colorado.gov/conservation/noxious-weeds/noxious-weed-management-in-colorado (list management goals)
- Rules: 8 CCR 1206-2 (via https://www.landcan.org/pdfs/coloradoNoxiousWeedAct.pdf — Part 6, Watch List species)

## 3. What listing would trigger in Colorado (Colorado Noxious Weed Act, C.R.S. 35-5.5-101 et seq.)

- **Duty of all persons:** "It is the duty of ALL persons to use integrated methods to manage
  noxious weeds if the same are likely to be materially damaging to the land of neighboring
  landowners."
- **County plans:** every county must adopt and enforce a noxious weed management plan; counties
  appoint weed managers; enforcement can begin from inspections, citizen complaints, or warrants.
- **List A/B on private land:** mandatory eradication/containment on ALL public and private lands;
  it is a violation to allow listed species to produce seed or reproduce.
- **If a landowner does not comply:** after notice (and an arbitration-panel option), the local
  governing body may enter and perform the weed management itself, then assess the whole cost
  (plus up to 20% for inspection/incidentals) as a **lien against the property**, with priority
  over all liens except general taxes and prior special assessments; collectible through the
  county treasurer like taxes. The local body must first apply the same or greater measures to
  its own adjacent lands/rights-of-way.
- **Civil penalties:** $500–$1,000 per violation; each day of continued violation is a separate
  offense (up to $100/day continuing penalty); penalties become liens collectible like taxes.
- **Sale/grow ban:** illegal to sell, offer for sale, or intentionally grow any List A/B/C species.
- Compliance waivers are available from the Commissioner where eradication is not practical for a
  specific population.

## 4. Spotted lanternfly (Lycorma delicatula) — proximity to Colorado

- **Not in Colorado.** Colorado Department of Agriculture: "So far, no sightings have been
  confirmed as the SLF in Colorado." CDA runs a Cooperative Agricultural Pest Survey and asks the
  public to report suspect sightings/egg masses.
- **Nearest confirmed detections:** **Iowa** — Des Moines County (southeast Iowa; found September
  in an industrial area with heavy out-of-state truck traffic) and an earlier Dallas County
  detection (2022, apple orchard; no established infestation found). **Illinois** — Cook County
  (Chicago area; first confirmed September 2023). No confirmed detections reported in Kansas,
  Nebraska, or other states bordering Colorado in sources reviewed.
- CDA notes SLF adults "prefer to lay eggs and are commonly found in high numbers on Black Walnut
  and Ailanthus trees (aka tree of heaven)" — the host-plant link.
- Context: first US detection 2014 (southeastern PA); now in 15+ eastern/midwestern states;
  first Canadian detection confirmed Sept 15, 2026 (Windsor, Ontario).

## 5. Denver municipal vegetation/abatement powers

Denver's Neighborhood Inspection Services (ZNIS), City and County of Denver:

- **Owner duties:** property owners must maintain their private property AND the adjacent public
  right-of-way (tree lawn, alley to the center line). Applies to owners, agents, and lessees.
- **What counts as a violation:** vegetation that poses a fire hazard; attracts pests (rodents,
  insects, wildlife); blocks sightlines; or includes noxious weeds regulated under state law.
  Generally, vegetation taller than 6 inches is considered overgrown (unless intentionally planted
  and well-maintained). Owners must also treat pest-/disease-stricken plants and remove dead plants.
- **Enforcement ladder:** (1) notice of violation by mail, in person, or posting (effective 5 days
  after mailing/posting); (2) 10 days to correct — the notice stays valid for one year, so repeat
  violations need no new notice; (3) if not fixed, **city inspectors may enter the property,
  clear the vegetation, and bill the owner** (removal + hauling + disposal); unpaid bills can
  become a **lien on the property**.
- **Entry:** if the vegetation is visible from the street, sidewalk, or a neighboring property,
  the city generally does not need a warrant; a warrant is required for fully enclosed/locked
  properties or if the owner asks inspectors to leave. Staff may only remove vegetation debris and
  related pests — not enter occupied buildings.
- **Appeals:** within 10 days (notice of violation) or 30 days (abatement bill); filing fee applies.
- **Key nuance for tree of heaven:** because it is NOT a state-listed noxious weed, Denver's
  "noxious weeds" violation hook does not apply to it. Applicable hooks would be fire hazard,
  pest attraction, sightline obstruction, or the 6-inch rule (root suckers/saplings).
- **State backstop:** C.R.S. § 31-15-401(1)(d) authorizes municipalities to levy priority liens
  for weed/brush/rubbish removal costs (strengthened by SB 23-052: the lien survives issuance of
  a treasurer's deed). http://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/92961/download
